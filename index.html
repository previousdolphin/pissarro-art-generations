Art Generations

We take the paintings Camille Pissarro left behind — the light on the Seine, the paths of English gardens, the hush of a Paris afternoon — and pass them through generative models trained to think in his hand. What returns is not a copy but a generation: a plausible cousin of the original, built to sit beside it and ask what else the scene might have become.

About the Organization

Pissarro Art Generations is a small archival project, not a studio. We do not paint over Camille Pissarro's work, and we do not claim these images as his. Instead we build and run generative models trained closely on his surviving canvases — the Kew garden studies, the Paris riverside and boulevard series, the harvest and orchard scenes of Pontoise and Éragny — and ask them to produce plausible variations: alternate light, alternate weather, alternate angles a nineteenth-century Impressionist might have painted on a different afternoon.

Every generation in the library is logged against the original it descended from, so the lineage stays visible rather than invented. Our aim is scholarly curiosity more than novelty — a way of sitting with an Impressionist's habits of light and brushwork long enough that a machine can begin to imagine in the same register.

The library will grow slowly and deliberately. Each new generation is reviewed against the source painting before it is added, and variants are kept in pairs or series where useful, as with the two current readings of the Pont Royal on the full portfolio page.
