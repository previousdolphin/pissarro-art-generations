The Portfolio

Every generation currently in circulation, catalogued in the order it was processed. Seventeen images now, ranging from Pissarro's crowded Paris boulevards and quiet Seine embankments to the market squares of Pontoise, the orchards and church of Éragny, the harvest at Montfoucault, and the industrial haze of Rouen harbor — including a divergent pair run from the same source painting under different settings, kept side by side so the two readings can be studied against one another.

Catalogue

The Library, in Full

Each entry is logged against the original painting it descended from, with a note on how the generation was set.

Generation No. 01 · Source: Kew, The Path to the Main Conservatory

The Path to the Main Conservatory

A model retrained on Pissarro's late garden studies extends the avenue of Kew a little further into the mist, keeping his loose, weather-worn brushwork intact while imagining the walk beyond the frame.

Notes: high-fidelity texture pass, foliage weighting increased to preserve the density of his brush.

Generation No. 02 · Source: Quai Malaquais in the Afternoon Sunshine, 1903

Quai Malaquais, Afternoon Sunshine

The last year of Pissarro's life produced some of his sunniest Paris views; this generation pushes the warmth of that 1903 afternoon further into the stone and the water, testing how far the light could travel before the scene stopped feeling like his.

Notes: warm-channel bias raised, architectural edges left soft to match the original's late-period looseness.

Generation No. 03 · Source: The Pont Royal and the Pavillon de Flore, 1903

Pont Royal & Pavillon de Flore — Variant A

The first of two readings of this Seine-side view, favouring the cooler blues Pissarro reached for in overcast Paris, with the Pavillon de Flore held in soft focus behind the traffic on the bridge.

Notes: cool-channel default, low contrast on the pavillion to keep the bridge as the focal plane.

Generation No. 04 · Source: The Pont Royal and the Pavillon de Flore, 1903

Pont Royal & Pavillon de Flore — Variant B

A second pass on the same bridge, run with the model set to favour warmer sunlight and a lower vantage point — kept beside its sibling so the two generations can be compared as a single divergent pair.

Notes: warm-channel bias, vantage lowered roughly a third, paired deliberately with Generation No. 03.

Generation No. 05 · Source: Boulevard Montmartre, Morning, Winter Sunshine, 1897

Boulevard Montmartre, Winter Morning

Pissarro painted this crowded avenue from a hotel window over a dozen times in different light; this generation follows his elevated vantage down into the low winter sun, letting the crowd blur into hurried strokes the way his own hand did from above.

Notes: vantage locked to third-floor height, crowd density increased slightly to match the 1897 series' bustle.

Generation No. 06 · Source: Boulevard Montmartre at Night, 1897

Boulevard Montmartre, After Dark

One of only a handful of nocturnes Pissarro attempted, this generation follows his gaslit streets and rain-slicked cobbles, letting the model find its own balance between the electric glow of shopfronts and the deep indigo dark he preferred over black.

Notes: shadow channel weighted toward indigo rather than neutral black, per the source painting's unusual palette.

Generation No. 07 · Source: Rue Saint-Honoré in the Afternoon, Effect of Rain, 1897

Rue Saint-Honoré, Rain Effect

Pissarro was fascinated by how rain flattened a city's palette; this generation follows suit, softening the avenue's traffic into wet reflections while keeping the awnings and rooftops legible enough to place the street.

Notes: reflection pass doubled, edge sharpness reduced across the lower third to mimic wet pavement.

Generation No. 08 · Source: The Pork Butcher, Pontoise Marketplace, 1883

Pontoise, the Marketplace

A busier, more populated generation than most in the library, this reading leans into the rural commerce Pissarro documented at Pontoise — stalls, baskets, and figures caught mid-transaction under an overcast sky.

Notes: figure count increased over the source painting, faces deliberately left indistinct.

Generation No. 09 · Source: Entrance to the Village of Voisins, 1872

Entrance to the Village of Voisins

An early, quieter Pissarro subject, this generation keeps to the flatter, more disciplined brushwork of his pre-Impressionist years, following the road as it curves toward rooftops just visible through bare trees.

Notes: brush texture set to an earlier, tighter setting to match the 1872 source.

Generation No. 10 · Source: Hoar Frost, the Old Road to Ennery, 1873

Hoar Frost, the Old Road to Ennery

Pissarro's winter scenes rely on colour more than white paint to describe frost; this generation follows that logic, building the rime on the furrows out of pale lavenders and blues rather than flat highlights.

Notes: white point suppressed, cool colour range widened to carry the frost.

Generation No. 11 · Source: The Hillside, Pontoise, 1875

The Hillside at Pontoise

A study in Pissarro's structured, almost geometric approach to the Pontoise farmland — terraced fields and orchard rows generated to hold the same disciplined perspective as his own hillside canvases.

Notes: perspective grid enforced during generation to preserve the terracing.

Generation No. 12 · Source: The Church at Éragny, 1884

The Church at Éragny

A modest village church rendered in the loosened, more broken brushwork Pissarro developed after his move to Éragny — this generation keeps that later looseness rather than reverting to his earlier, tighter hand.

Notes: brush looseness increased two settings above the library default to match his Éragny period.

Generation No. 13 · Source: Peasants' Houses, Éragny, 1887

Peasants' Houses, Éragny

Domestic rather than grand, this generation follows Pissarro's late interest in the everyday architecture of rural Normandy, keeping the thatch and stone walls slightly unresolved the way his own late work left them.

Notes: architectural edges intentionally under-resolved, consistent with the source's late-period looseness.

Generation No. 14 · Source: Apple Picking at Éragny-sur-Epte, 1888

Apple Picking at Éragny-sur-Epte

One of Pissarro's most pointillist-adjacent works, made during his brief flirtation with Seurat and Signac's methods; this generation experiments with a tighter, dotted brush setting rarely used elsewhere in the library.

Notes: stipple/dot brush mode enabled specifically for this generation, matching Pissarro's Neo-Impressionist period.

Generation No. 15 · Source: The Harvest at Montfoucault, 1876

The Harvest at Montfoucault

A rare rural genre scene from Pissarro's years visiting Brittany, this generation follows the gathered figures and stacked hay with warmer, earthier tones than the cooler Pontoise and Éragny work elsewhere in the archive.

Notes: warm-earth palette substituted for the library's default cool bias, to match the Montfoucault source.

Generation No. 16 · Source: The Gleaners, 1889

The Gleaners

Pissarro's stooping field-workers, generated with an emphasis on the low horizon and long shadow he used to give the figures monumental weight against an otherwise plain field.

Notes: horizon line placed low deliberately, per the source, to keep the figures visually dominant.

Generation No. 17 · Source: The Seine at Rouen, 1898

The Seine at Rouen

The final geographic pivot in the current library, this generation follows Pissarro's Rouen harbor series into its industrial haze — smokestacks and masts rendered with the same soft, atmospheric blur he used to unify the busy waterfront.

Notes: atmospheric haze increased across the upper third, masts and rigging kept slightly sharper as a focal anchor.
